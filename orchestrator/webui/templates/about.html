About MRAG

MRAG — Master Regulator analysis of Accessible Genome. Give it the ATAC-seq and RNA-seq of a two-condition experiment and it ranks the transcription factors that drive the difference, builds their super-enhancer → factor → target network, finds literature evidence for the targets and writes an interactive report.

What is automated, and what the language models do

The analysis is a fixed pipeline: which steps run follows from the inputs you supply and from run.toml, never from a language model. Local LLMs score and audit the literature evidence for candidate target genes, judge which enrichment terms fit the lineage, and write the report text. The chat assistant chooses among a few chat actions: detect samples, start a run, show status, open a report, add a locus track, and SRA lookup.

  • Reads your sample layout. Infers modalities, pairs replicates, proposes the comparison.
  • Picks the right reference and propagates the sign convention end-to-end.
  • Filters pathway noise using dataset context (cell line, tissue, perturbation).
  • Writes the narrative grounded in your numbers and cited literature, not templates.
  • Recovers from failure. Checkpointed, diagnosable, resumable.

What it can call on

Standard genomics methods, run in a fixed order. Which of them run depends on the stage your data enter at (FASTQ, peaks or matrices):

  • Chromatin — bowtie2 + MACS2 peaks, DAStk differential motif activity, DiffBind for binding-level statistics.
  • Expression — STAR + RSEM + DESeq2 (R engine), ENSG→symbol resolution against GENCODE v34.
  • Integration — Stouffer Z combination of ATAC and RNA p-values, producing a deterministic INTEGRATED_ranked.csv with explicit per-TF rank.
  • Regulatory wiring — HOMER motif annotation (JASPAR-extended), Cytoscape bipartite TRN graph, per-TF average ATAC profile, pyGenomeTracks locus snapshots.
  • In silico validation — the network's super-enhancer → factor edges tested in sequence space with AlphaGenome and Corgi: motif scrambles against size-matched control edits, scored against lineage-foreign factors. Factor → target edges are measured too and stay motif-based candidates.
  • Interpretation — Functional enrichment (Hallmark + GO BP + Reactome) with LLM relevance filtering; PubMed-grounded narrative authored by Qwen3.6-27B.

What you get back

  • A ranked roster of master-regulator TFs with direction (gain vs loss of activity), statistical support, and motif evidence.
  • An interactive TRN graph showing how those TFs converge on shared target programs.
  • Locus-level snapshots that anchor TF→target pairs in real chromatin tracks across all replicates.
  • Functional enrichment of the convergent target program, filtered for biological plausibility in your specific context.
  • An in silico test of the network's super-enhancer → factor edges with AlphaGenome and Corgi, reported at the pair level with its own statistics.
  • A single HTML report with its figures embedded (the interactive parts load their JavaScript libraries from public CDNs) and a provenance manifest that records each step's input hash, parameters and outputs.

Section 13: what a run declares

The in silico axis section always prints all three model sets (AlphaGenome, Corgi, both) at the pair level. Which one it leads with — the figure's first tab and the emphasised row — is declared by the run, never picked by the smallest p:

  • axis_headline (top-level key of run.toml; the confirm form's “Leading model set”): auto (key absent) applies the default rule — the consensus leads when both arms ran and both single-model headline tests survive Holm; both, alphagenome or corgi declare it.
  • axis_ar_gate (true/false; the form's “AR cross-condition row”): adds the AR-gated enhancer row, which then becomes that model's headline row. A declared PRAD-only extra requirement, off by default.
  • A bulk prostate-adenocarcinoma (PRAD) tumour cohort declares axis_headline = "alphagenome" and axis_ar_gate = true; the form pre-selects them when the run names prostate adenocarcinoma, and you decide. OC2 and cell-line runs keep auto.

When MRAG is the right agent for the job

  • You have paired ATAC-seq + RNA-seq for a 2-arm comparison (perturbation vs control, tumor vs normal, etc.).
  • You want to know which TFs are driving the differential program — not just a DEG list.
  • You want a publication-ready interactive report from raw FASTQ without scripting the pipeline yourself.
